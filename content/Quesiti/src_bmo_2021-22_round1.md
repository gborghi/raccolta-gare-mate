*Find smallest possible number of table tennis games played*

> One day Arun and Disha played several games of table tennis. At five points during the day, Arun calculated the percentage of the games played so far that he had won. The results of these calculations were exactly $30\%$, exactly $40\%$, exactly $50\%$, exactly $60\%$ and exactly $70\%$ in some order. What is the smallest possible number of games they could have played?

*Prove triangle DOE is equilateral given two intersecting circles*

> Two circles $\Gamma_1$ and $\Gamma_2$ have centres $O_1$ and $O_2$ respectively. They pass through each other's centres and intersect at $A$ and $B$. The point $C$ lies on the minor arc $BO_2$ of $\Gamma_1$. The points $D$ and $E$ lie on the line $O_2C$ such that $\angle AO_1D = \angle DO_1C$ and $\angle CO_1E = \angle EO_1B$. Prove that triangle $DOE$ is equilateral.
> 
> (A minor arc of a circle is the shorter of the two arcs with given endpoints.)

**Topic:** [[topic_geometria_piana|Geometria piana]]
**Metodo:** [[method_trigonometria|Trigonometria]], [[method_simmetria|Simmetria]]
**Abilita:** [[skill_ragionamento_geometrico|Ragionamento geometrico]], [[skill_manipolazione_algebrica|Manipolazione algebrica]], [[skill_astrazione|Astrazione]]

*Dimostrare che il triangolo DOE è equilatero dati due cerchi che si intersecano*

> Due circonferenze $\Gamma_1$ e $\Gamma_2$ hanno centri rispettivamente in $O_1$ e $O_2$. Passano per i centri l'una dell'altra e si intersecano in $A$ e $B$. Il punto $C$ giace sull'arco minore $BO_2$ della circonferenza $\Gamma_1$. I punti $D$ e $E$ giacciono sulla retta $O_2C$ in modo che $\angle AO_1D = \angle DO_1C$ e $\angle CO_1E = \angle EO_1B$. Dimostrare che il triangolo $DOE$ è equilatero.
>
> (Un arco minore di una circonferenza è il più breve dei due archi aventi gli estremi dati.)

*Count lists of 71 integers summing to 999,999 with given recurrence*

> Marvin has been tasked with writing down every list of integers with the following properties:
> 
> (i) The list contains 71 terms.
> 
> (ii) The first term is $1$.
> 
> (iii) Every term after the first is equal to either the previous term, or the sum of all previous terms.
> 
> When Marvin is finished, how many of the lists will have a sum equal to $999{,}999$?
