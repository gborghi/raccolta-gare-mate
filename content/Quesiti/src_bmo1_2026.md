*Express p^n as sum of a ramp*

> A *ramp* is a sequence of three different positive integers $a, b, c$ such that $a$ is a factor of $b$ and $b$ is a factor of $c$. For every prime number $p$ and every positive integer $n$, determine with proof whether $p^n$ can be expressed as the sum of a ramp.

*Count ways to reassemble 11x11 grid from L-shaped pieces*

> An $11 \times 11$ square grid is dissected into 11 pieces as follows. At each step, remove the top row and the leftmost column of the remaining grid as a single piece. Thus, the pieces obtained are L-shaped with sizes $21, 19, \ldots, 3$, together with a single square.
> 
> The pieces are rearranged to reassemble an $11 \times 11$ grid, with rotations allowed (but the pieces may not be turned over) so that the single square occupies the cell in the third row and third column (counting from the top left).
> 
> How many ways can this be done?

*Sequence with halving/rounding rules; does it hit every positive integer?*

> George defines a sequence of positive integers $t_1, t_2, t_3, \ldots$ as follows.
> 
> He first sets $t_1 = 1$. Then, for $n \ge 1$:
> \begin{itemize}
> \item If $t_n$ is even, then $t_{n+1} = t_n / 2$.
> \item If $t_n$ is odd and greater than 1, then $t_{n+1} = t_n / 3$ rounded to the nearest integer.
> \item If $t_n = 1$, then $t_{n+1} = 2025k$ where $k$ is the number of terms equal to $1$ amongst $t_1, t_2, \ldots, t_n$.
> \end{itemize}
> Does this sequence contain every positive integer?

*Frogs on 1000 lily pads; find n for exactly one frog remaining*

> There are 1000 lily pads on a pond arranged in a circle and labelled $1, 2, \ldots, 1000$ in order. (The first four lily pads may also be described using the labels 1001, 1002, 1003 and 1004, respectively.) The first $n$ lily pads are each occupied by a frog with the remaining lily pads not occupied.
> 
> Each minute, exactly one of the frogs makes a move. Suppose the frog is on lily pad $k$. That frog may either:
> 
> (i) Swim to lily pad $k + 4$ or $k - 4$, provided that it is not occupied; or
> 
> (ii) Jump to lily pad $k + 3$ or $k - 3$ provided that this lily pad is not occupied and the two lily pads jumped over are both occupied. When this happens, the two frogs that were jumped over dive into the pond and don't participate in any further moves.
> 
> For which values of $n$ is it possible, by a sequence of moves, to end with exactly one frog remaining on the lily pads?

*Per quali $n$ si può restare con una sola rana sulle ninfee*

> Su uno stagno ci sono 1000 ninfee disposte in cerchio ed etichettate $1, 2, \ldots, 1000$ in ordine. (Le prime quattro ninfee si possono anche descrivere con le etichette 1001, 1002, 1003 e 1004, rispettivamente.) Le prime $n$ ninfee sono occupate ciascuna da una rana, e le altre sono libere.
>
> Ogni minuto esattamente una delle rane fa una mossa. Si supponga che la rana sia sulla ninfea $k$. Quella rana può:
>
> (i) nuotare alla ninfea $k + 4$ o $k - 4$, purché non sia occupata; oppure
>
> (ii) saltare alla ninfea $k + 3$ o $k - 3$, purché questa ninfea non sia occupata e le due ninfee scavalcate siano entrambe occupate. In questo caso le due rane scavalcate si tuffano nello stagno e non partecipano ad altre mosse.
>
> Per quali valori di $n$ è possibile, con una successione di mosse, finire con esattamente una rana rimasta sulle ninfee?
