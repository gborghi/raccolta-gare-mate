*Fibonacci sequence: show f_n - ab^n divisible by all positive integers*

> The Fibonacci sequence $\{f_n\}$ is defined by
> $$f_1 = 1,\quad f_2 = 1,\quad f_{n+2} = f_{n+1} + f_n \qquad (n > 2).$$
> Prove that there are unique integers $a$, $b$ such that $0 < a < b$ and $f_n - ab^n$ is divisible by $b$ for all positive integers $n$.

*Sequence x_n with x_1=-1, x_2=2; prove nS_n + F_n = 1 for all n*

> The real numbers $x_1, x_2, x_3, \ldots$ are defined by
> $$x_1 = -1, \quad x_2 = 2 \quad \text{and} \quad x_{n+1} = 2x_n - x_{n-1} \text{ for all } n \geq 1.$$
> $S_n$ is the mean and $F_n$ is the $n$-th term of the sequence $y_1, y_2, \ldots$, where $y_n = \dfrac{x_{n-1} + x_n}{2}$.
> 
> Prove that $nS_n + F_n = 1$ for all $n$.

*Cupola on tower: find shortest path over surface*

> The two cylindrical surfaces
> $$x^2 + z^2 = 4, \quad y \geq 0 \quad \text{and} \quad y^2 + z^2 = 4, \quad x \geq 0$$
> intersect and with $x \geq 0$, $y \geq 0$, $z \geq 0$ form a dome-like shape which is here called a "cupola". The cupola is placed on top of a vertical tower of height $h$ whose horizontal cross-section is a square of side $2$. Find the shortest distance from the highest point of the cupola to a corner of the base of the tower, over the surface of the cupola and tower.

*Equation with square roots; find p for exactly one real solution x*

> Consider the equation
> $$\sqrt{2p + 1 - x^2} + \sqrt{3x + p + 4} = \sqrt{x^2 + 9x + 3p + 9} \tag{1}$$
> in which $x, p$ are real numbers and the square roots are to be real and non-negative. Show that if $(1)$ holds then
> $$(x^2 + x - p)(x^2 + 8x + 2p + 9) = 0.$$
> Hence find the set of real numbers $p$ for which $(1)$ is satisfied by exactly one real number $x$.

**Metodo:** [[method_casework|Casework]], [[method_fattorizzazione|Fattorizzazione]]
**Abilita:** [[skill_manipolazione_algebrica|Manipolazione algebrica]], [[skill_lettura_attenta|Lettura attenta]], [[skill_casework_accurato|Casework accurato]]
**Area:** [[Algebra e Analisi]]
**Fonte:** [apri PDF](https://drive.google.com/file/d/13blbg3MFhT-UVgIheFrX8WBP_TBbhjZm/view)


<div class="qlang-split" data-lang="it"></div>


*L'insieme dei $p$ reali per cui (1) è soddisfatta da esattamente un $x$ reale*

> Si consideri l'equazione
> $$\sqrt{2p + 1 - x^2} + \sqrt{3x + p + 4} = \sqrt{x^2 + 9x + 3p + 9} \tag{1}$$
> in cui $x, p$ sono numeri reali e le radici quadrate devono essere reali e non negative. Dimostrare che se vale $(1)$ allora
> $$(x^2 + x - p)(x^2 + 8x + 2p + 9) = 0.$$
> Trovare dunque l'insieme dei numeri reali $p$ per cui $(1)$ è soddisfatta da esattamente un numero reale $x$.
