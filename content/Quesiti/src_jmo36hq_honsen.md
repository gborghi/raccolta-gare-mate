*Acute triangle, circumcenter, points on ray DO; find angle BAC*

> Let $ABC$ be an acute triangle with $AB < AC$, let $O$ be its circumcenter, and let $D$ be the intersection point of the bisector of $\angle BAC$ with side $BC$. On the ray $DO$ take two distinct points $P, Q$, both lying on the opposite side of line $AC$ from $B$ and on the same side of line $AB$ as $C$, so that the three points $D, P, Q$ lie in this order. Moreover, $AB = BP$, $AC = CQ$, and $\angle ABP = \angle ACQ$ hold. Find the value of $\angle BAC$. Here, $XY$ denotes the length of segment $XY$.

*Un valore in un triangolo acutangolo, dati circocentro e bisettrice*

Sulla semiretta $DO$ si prendano due punti distinti $P, Q$, entrambi dalla parte opposta della retta $AC$ rispetto a $B$ e dalla stessa parte della retta $AB$ di $C$, in modo che i tre punti $D, P, Q$ stiano in quest'ordine. Inoltre valgono $AB = BP$, $AC = CQ$ e $\angle ABP = \angle ACQ$. Trovare il valore di $\angle BAC$. Qui $XY$ indica la lunghezza del segmento $XY$.

*Sequence of odd numbers defined by coprimality; bound |a_n - 2n|*

> A sequence $a_1, a_2, \ldots$ consisting of positive odd integers satisfies, for every positive integer $n$, the following condition:
> 
> The smallest odd integer that is greater than $a_{n+1}$ and coprime to $a_n$ is $a_{n+2}$.
> 
> Prove that there exists a positive integer $C$ such that $|a_n - 2n| \leq C$ holds for every positive integer $n$.

*Regular m-gon, n red and n blue vertices, no matching red/blue distance; find min m*

> Let $n$ be an integer with $n \geq 2$ and let $m$ be an integer with $m \geq 2n$. Choose $2n$ distinct vertices of a regular $m$-gon, color $n$ of them red and the remaining $n$ blue, so that the following holds:
> 
> No matter how one chooses two distinct red points and two distinct blue points, the distance between the two red points and the distance between the two blue points do not coincide.
> 
> Determine, in terms of $n$, the smallest possible value of $m$ for which such a coloring exists.

*Point P off the plane with equal products; circumcircles meet plane in triangle similar to T*

> In three-dimensional space there is a plane $\pi$. On $\pi$ there is a non-isosceles triangle $ABC$, and let $I$ be its incenter. Prove that there exists a triangle $\mathcal{T}$ such that, for every point $P$ not lying on $\pi$ satisfying
> $$AP \cdot BC = BP \cdot CA = CP \cdot AB,$$
> the following holds:
> 
> Let $I_A$, $I_B$, $I_C$ be the incenters of triangles $BCP$, $CAP$, $ABP$, respectively. If the circumcircle of triangle $II_BI_C$, the circumcircle of triangle $II_CI_A$, and the circumcircle of triangle $II_AI_B$ meet $\pi$ at points $X$, $Y$, $Z$ respectively (each different from $I$), then triangle $XYZ$ is similar to $\mathcal{T}$.
> 
> Here, $UV$ denotes the length of segment $UV$.

*Esiste un triangolo simile a un altro, per ogni punto fuori dal piano*

> Nello spazio tridimensionale c'è un piano $\pi$. Su $\pi$ c'è un triangolo non isoscele $ABC$, e sia $I$ il suo incentro. Dimostrare che esiste un triangolo $\mathcal{T}$ tale che, per ogni punto $P$ che non sta su $\pi$ e soddisfa
>
> $$AP \cdot BC = BP \cdot CA = CP \cdot AB,$$
>
> valga quanto segue.
>
> Siano $I_A$, $I_B$, $I_C$ gli incentri dei triangoli $BCP$, $CAP$, $ABP$ rispettivamente. Se la circonferenza circoscritta al triangolo $II_BI_C$, la circonferenza circoscritta al triangolo $II_CI_A$ e la circonferenza circoscritta al triangolo $II_AI_B$ incontrano $\pi$ rispettivamente nei punti $X$, $Y$, $Z$ (ciascuno diverso da $I$), allora il triangolo $XYZ$ è simile a $\mathcal{T}$.
>
> Qui $UV$ indica la lunghezza del segmento $UV$.
