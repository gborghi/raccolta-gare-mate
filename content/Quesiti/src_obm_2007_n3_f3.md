*Prove the set of side and diagonal lengths of a convex n-gon has at least floor(n/2) distinct values*

> $n$ points are given in the plane, which are the vertices of a convex polygon. Prove that the set of measures of the sides and diagonals of the polygon has at least $\lfloor n/2 \rfloor$ distinct elements.
> 
> *Remark:* $\lfloor x \rfloor$ denotes the greatest integer that does not exceed $x$. For example, $\lfloor 2.5 \rfloor = 2$, $\lfloor 3 \rfloor = 3$ and $\lfloor -1.2 \rfloor = -2$.

*Game on a 2007x2007 board: Arnaldo removes 2x2 blocks, Bernaldo removes single squares; can Bernaldo always win?*

> $2007^2$ equal little squares are arranged forming a $2007 \times 2007$ board. Arnaldo and Bernaldo play the following game: each of Arnaldo's moves consists of removing 4 little squares that form a $2 \times 2$ square. Each of Bernaldo's moves consists of removing only 1 little square. The players play alternately, with Arnaldo being the first to play. When Arnaldo can no longer make his move, Bernaldo takes for himself all the little squares remaining on the board at the end. The one who has more little squares wins. Is it possible for Bernaldo to win, no matter how Arnaldo plays?

Quando Arnaldo non può più compiere la propria mossa, Bernaldo si prende tutti i quadratini che restano sulla scacchiera alla fine. Vince chi ha più quadratini. È possibile che Bernaldo vinca, indipendentemente da come gioca Arnaldo?

*Reals where each value occurs at most twice among pairwise differences; prove at least floor(n/2) differences occur exactly once*

> Given real numbers $x_1 \le x_2 \le \ldots \le x_n$, suppose that every number occurs at most twice among the differences $x_j - x_i$, $1 \le i < j \le n$. Prove that there are at least $\lfloor n/2 \rfloor$ real numbers that occur exactly once among such differences.
> 
> *Remark:* in case you do not remember it, on yesterday's exam, $\lfloor x \rfloor$ denotes the greatest integer that does not exceed $x$. For example, $\lfloor 2.5 \rfloor = 2$, $\lfloor 3 \rfloor = 3$ and $\lfloor -1.2 \rfloor = -2$.

*Reali in cui ciascun valore compare al più due volte tra le differenze a coppie; dimostrare che almeno floor(n/2) differenze compaiono esattamente una volta*

> Dati i numeri reali $x_1 \le x_2 \le \ldots \le x_n$, si supponga che, tra le differenze $x_j - x_i$, $1 \le i < j \le n$, ogni numero compaia al più due volte. Dimostrare che esistono almeno $\lfloor n/2 \rfloor$ numeri reali che compaiono esattamente una volta tra tali differenze.

> *Osservazione:* nel caso non te lo ricordassi, nell'esame di ieri, $\lfloor x \rfloor$ indica il più grande intero che non supera $x$. Ad esempio, $\lfloor 2.5 \rfloor = 2$, $\lfloor 3 \rfloor = 3$ e $\lfloor -1.2 \rfloor = -2$.
