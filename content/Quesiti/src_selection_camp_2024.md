*Complete graph of bridges partitioned into Hamiltonian-path companies; maximize companies*

> Let $n$ be an integer with $n \ge 2$. There are $n$ islands $I_1, I_2, \ldots, I_n$, and for each pair of distinct islands there is exactly one bridge (road) connecting them, usable in both directions. Each road is managed by exactly one of several road companies. The roads are managed so that one can travel between any two islands using only roads of a single company. Suppose that, for any road company, there is a permutation $p(1), p(2), \ldots, p(n)$ of $1, 2, \ldots, n$ such that that company manages exactly the roads joining $I_{p(i)}$ and $I_{p(i+1)}$ for $i = 1, 2, \ldots, n-1$ (i.e. each company's roads form a Hamiltonian path on the $n$ islands). Determine the maximum possible number of road companies. Here a permutation $p(1), p(2), \ldots, p(n)$ of $1, 2, \ldots, n$ means each integer from $1$ to $n$ appears exactly once.

*Grafo completo di ponti suddiviso in compagnie a percorso di Hamilton; massimizza le compagnie*

> Sia $n$ un numero intero tale che $n \ge 2$. Ci sono $n$ isole $I_1, I_2, \ldots, I_n$, e per ogni coppia di isole distinte esiste esattamente un ponte (strada) che le collega, utilizzabile in entrambe le direzioni. Ogni strada è gestita da esattamente una tra diverse compagnie stradali. Le strade sono gestite in modo che si possa viaggiare tra due isole qualsiasi utilizzando solo strade di una singola compagnia. Supponiamo che, per ogni compagnia stradale, esista una permutazione $p(1), p(2), \ldots, p(n)$ di $1, 2, \ldots, n$ tale che quella compagnia gestisca esattamente le strade che collegano $I_{p(i)}$ e $I_{p(i+1)}$ per $i = 1, 2, \ldots, n-1$ (ossia le strade di ciascuna compagnia formano un cammino di Hamilton sulle $n$ isole). Determinare il numero massimo possibile di compagnie stradali. Qui, una permutazione $p(1), p(2), \ldots, p(n)$ di $1, 2, \ldots, n$ significa che ogni numero intero da $1$ a $n$ compare esattamente una volta.

*Longest sequence of integers in [1,2^2023] with no zero signed subsum*

> Find the largest positive integer $N$ such that there exists a sequence $a_1, a_2, \ldots, a_N$ satisfying both of the following:
> \begin{itemize}
> \item For every integer $i$ with $1 \le i \le N$, $a_i$ is an integer with $1 \le a_i \le 2^{2023}$.
> \item For all integers $i, j$ with $1 \le i \le j \le N$ and every choice of $s_i, s_{i+1}, \ldots, s_j$ each equal to $1$ or $-1$,
> $$s_i a_i + s_{i+1} a_{i+1} + \cdots + s_j a_j \neq 0.$$
> \end{itemize}

*Three permutations of 1..n approximating 2 sqrt(n) by sums of square roots*

> Let $n$ be a positive integer. Prove that there exist permutations $(a_1, a_2, \ldots, a_n)$, $(b_1, b_2, \ldots, b_n)$, $(c_1, c_2, \ldots, c_n)$ of $(1, 2, \ldots, n)$ such that for every integer $k$ with $1 \le k \le n$,
