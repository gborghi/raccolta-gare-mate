*Fibonacci-like integer sequence: infinitely many integers are non-fibonacciano*

> For a positive integer $n$, define $F^{(0)}_n = n$, $F^{(1)}_n = n$ and, for $p > 2$:
> $$F^{(p)}_n = F^{(p-1)}_n + F^{(p-1)}_{n+1}.$$
> A positive integer $a$ is called *fibonacciano* if $F^{(p)}_a = a$ for some positive integer $p$ and some $n > 3$. Prove that there exist infinitely many positive integers that are not fibonacciano.

*Successione intera di tipo Fibonacci: infiniti interi sono non-fibonacciani*

> Per un intero positivo $n$, si definiscano $F^{(0)}_n = n$, $F^{(1)}_n = n$ e, per $p > 2$:
> $$F^{(p)}_n = F^{(p-1)}_n + F^{(p-1)}_{n+1}.$$
> Un numero intero positivo $a$ è detto *fibonacciano* se $F^{(p)}_a = a$ per qualche numero intero positivo $p$ e qualche $n > 3$. Dimostrare che esistono infiniti numeri interi positivi che non sono fibonacciani.

*Mixtilinear circles in a triangle: common tangents concurrent and isogonal conjugates*

> Let $r_A$, $r_B$, $r_C$ be the mixtilinear inradii of triangle $XYZ$ centered at $P$ in the interior of the triangle. The circle $\omega_A$ (center $X$) is internally tangent to $r_B r_C$; the circle $\omega_B$ (center $Y$) is internally tangent to $r_C r_A$; and the circle $\omega_C$ (center $Z$) is internally tangent to $r_A r_B$. The point $P$ lies in the interior of triangle $XYZ$ such that the lines $r_A$, $r_B$, $r_C$ are the common internal tangents to the corresponding pairs of circles. Let $s_A$ be the line internally tangent to $\omega_B$ and $\omega_C$ that does not contain $r_A$; let $s_B$ be the line internally tangent to $\omega_A$ and $\omega_C$ that does not contain $r_B$; let $s_C$ be the line internally tangent to $\omega_A$ and $\omega_B$ that does not contain $r_C$. Prove that $s_A$, $s_B$, and $s_C$ meet at a common point $Q$, and that $P$ and $Q$ are isogonal conjugates in triangle $XYZ$ — that is, the lines $XP$ and $XQ$ are symmetric with respect to the angle bisector of $\angle YXZ$, and similarly for vertices $Y$ and $Z$.

*Cerchi mistilinei in un triangolo: tangenti comuni concorrenti e coniugati isogonali*

> Siano $r_A$, $r_B$, $r_C$ i raggi inradiati mistilineari del triangolo $XYZ$, centrati in $P$ all'interno del triangolo. Il cerchio $\omega_A$ (centro $X$) è tangente internamente a $r_B r_C$; il cerchio $\omega_B$ (centro $Y$) è tangente internamente a $r_C r_A$; e il cerchio $\omega_C$ (centro $Z$) è tangente internamente a $r_A r_B$. Il punto $P$ giace all'interno del triangolo $XYZ$ in modo tale che le rette $r_A$, $r_B$, $r_C$ siano le tangenti interne comuni alle coppie corrispondenti di cerchi. Sia $s_A$ la retta tangente internamente a $\omega_B$ e $\omega_C$ che non contiene $r_A$; sia $s_B$ la retta tangente internamente a $\omega_A$ e $\omega_C$ che non contiene $r_B$; sia $s_C$ la retta tangente internamente a $\omega_A$ e $\omega_B$ che non contiene $r_C$. Dimostrare che $s_A$, $s_B$ e $s_C$ si incontrano in un punto comune $Q$, e che $P$ e $Q$ sono coniugati isogonali nel triangolo $XYZ$ — vale a dire che le rette $XP$ e $XQ$ sono simmetriche rispetto alla bisettrice dell'angolo $\angle YXZ$, e analogamente per i vertici $Y$ e $Z$.

*Parity questions to identify truth-tellers in a group of n people*

> Let $n$ and $k$ be positive integers with $k \le n$. In a group of $n$ people, each person either always tells the truth or always lies. Arnaldo may ask questions of any of these people of the following type: given a subset $A$ of size $k$ of the group, "what is the parity of the number of truth-tellers in $A$?" The answer can only be "even" (par) or "odd" (ímpar).
> 
> (a) (4 points) For which values of $n$ and $k$ is it possible to determine, for each person, whether they always tell the truth or always lie?
> 
> (b) (6 points) What is the minimum number of questions necessary to make this determination, when it is possible?

*Iterates of f(x)=2x^2+x-1: real fixed points and real zeros*

> Let $f(x) = 2x^2 + x - 1$. Define $f^{n+1}(x) = f(f^n(x))$ for all real $x$ and $n \ge 0$ (that is, $f^n$ is the $n$-th iterate of $f$).
> 
> (a) (2 points) Determine the number of distinct real solutions of the equation $f^1(x) = x$.
> 
> (b) (8 points) Determine, for each non-negative integer $n$, the number of distinct real solutions of the equation $f^n(x) = 0$.
