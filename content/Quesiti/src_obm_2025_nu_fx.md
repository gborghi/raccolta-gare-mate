*Integer sequences; infinitely many N and M with ratio conditions*

> Determine whether there exist integers $a, b, c, d$ such that the sequences $(a_n)_{n \ge 0}$ and $(\beta_n)_{n \ge 0}$ defined by
> $$a_0 = \beta_0 = 0;$$
> $$a_1 = \beta_1 = 1;$$
> $$a_{n+2} = a\, a_{n+1} + b\, a_n \text{ for } n \ge 0;$$
> $$\beta_{n+2} = c\, \beta_{n+1} + d\, \beta_n \text{ for } n \ge 0;$$
> satisfy $\beta_n \ne 0$ for all $n \ge 1$, and both of the following conditions:
> 
> (i) There exist infinitely many natural numbers $N$ such that
> $$1 - \frac{1}{2^{2025}} < \left|\frac{a_N}{\beta_N}\right| < 1 + \frac{1}{2^{2025}}.$$
> 
> (ii) There exist infinitely many natural numbers $M$ such that
> $$\left|\frac{a_M}{\beta_M}\right| > 2^{2025}.$$

*Minimum-modulus elements under invertible linear maps on R^2*

> Given a nonempty set $X \subset \mathbb{R}^k$, $k \in \{2, 3\}$, we say that $q$ is of **minimum modulus** in $X$ if $q \in X$ and $|q| \le |p|$ for all $p \in X$.
> 
> (a) Let $A : \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$ be an invertible linear transformation, and let
> $$\Lambda = A(\mathbb{Z}^2) = \{A(x,y) \mid x, y \in \mathbb{Z}\}.$$
> Suppose $u$ is of minimum modulus in $\Lambda \setminus \{(0,0)\}$ and $v$ is of minimum modulus in $\Lambda \setminus \{tu \mid t \in \mathbb{R}\}$. Prove that $\Lambda = \{ku + mv \mid k, m \in \mathbb{Z}\}$.
> 
> (b) Let $B : \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$ be an invertible linear transformation, and let
> $$\Gamma = B(\mathbb{Z}^3) = \{B(x,y,z) \mid x, y, z \in \mathbb{Z}\}.$$
> Suppose $u$ is of minimum modulus in $\Gamma \setminus \{(0,0)\}$, $v$ is of minimum modulus in $\Gamma \setminus \{tu \mid t \in \mathbb{R}\}$, and $w$ is of minimum modulus in $\Gamma \setminus \{tu + sv \mid t, s \in \mathbb{R}\}$. Prove that $\Gamma = \{ku + mv + nw \mid k, m, n \in \mathbb{Z}\}$.

*Sequences of positive terms: divergence and convergence of series*

> (a) Let $(a_n)_{n \ge 1}$ be a decreasing sequence of positive terms such that the series $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{a_n}{n}$ diverges. Prove that for any increasing sequence $(n_k)_{k \ge 1}$ of positive integers with $\frac{n_{k+1}}{n_k}$ bounded, the series $\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} a_{n_k}$ diverges.
> 
> (b) Let $(a_n)_{n \ge 1}$ be a sequence of positive terms such that the series $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{a_n}{n}$ converges. Prove that there exists an increasing sequence $(n_k)_{k \ge 1}$ of positive integers with $\lim_{k \to \infty} \frac{n_{k+1}}{n_k} = 1$ such that the series $\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} a_{n_k}$ converges.

*Möbius maps on (-1,1) via tanh; find minimum contraction factor*

> (a) Let $g : \mathbb{R} \to (-1, 1)$ be the bijection given by
> $$g(x) = \tanh(x) = \frac{e^{2x} - 1}{e^{2x} + 1}.$$
> Given $r \in [0, 1)$, prove that there exists $\lambda(r) \in [0, 1)$ with the following property: for every rational function
> $$f(x) = \frac{ax + b}{cx + d}$$
> with $a, b, c, d$ real numbers and $|d| > |c|$ such that $f((-1,1)) \subset (-r, r)$, we have $|(g^{-1} \circ f \circ g)(x)| \le \lambda(r)$ for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$.
> 
> (b) Determine the smallest $\lambda = \lambda(r)$ possible in part (a).
