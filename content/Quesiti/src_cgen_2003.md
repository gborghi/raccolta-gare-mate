*Preliminary questions: a vector identity, concurrency of altitudes (orthocentre), and a characterization of the orthocentre via the circumcentre.*

> Questions preliminaires.
> 
> 1. Let $ABC$ be a triangle and $M$ an arbitrary point of the plane. Show that:
> $$\overrightarrow{MA}\cdot\overrightarrow{BC}+\overrightarrow{MB}\cdot\overrightarrow{CA}+\overrightarrow{MC}\cdot\overrightarrow{AB}=0.$$
> Deduce that the three altitudes of triangle $ABC$ are concurrent at a point $H$ called the orthocentre of the triangle.
> 
> 2. Let $ABC$ be a triangle, $\Omega$ the centre of its circumscribed circle and $H$ the point such that $\overrightarrow{\Omega H}=\overrightarrow{\Omega A}+\overrightarrow{\Omega B}+\overrightarrow{\Omega C}$. Show that $H$ is the orthocentre of triangle $ABC$.

*Questioni preliminari: un'identità vettoriale, la concomitanza delle altitudini (ortocentro) e una caratterizzazione dell'ortocentro attraverso il circoncentro.*

> Domande preliminari.
> 
> 1. Che $ABC$ sia un triangolo e $M$ un punto arbitrario del piano. Mostrare che: $$\overrightarrow{MA}\cdot\overrightarrow{BC}+\overrightarrow{MB}\cdot\overrightarrow{CA}+\overrightarrow{MC}\cdot\overrightarrow{AB}=0.$$ Deduzione che le tre altitudini del triangolo $ABC$ sono simultanee in un punto $H$ chiamato ortocentro del triangolo.
> 
> 2. Il $ABC$ è un triangolo, il $\Omega$ è il centro del suo cerchio circoscritto e il $H$ è il punto tale che $\overrightarrow{\Omega H}=\overrightarrow{\Omega A}+\overrightarrow{\Omega B}+\overrightarrow{\Omega C}$. Indicare che $H$ è l'ortocentro del triangolo $ABC$.

*Third part: coordinates of the orthocentre of a triangle with given coordinates; orthocentric set obtained from a line plus an external point; and properties of an orthocentric set contained in the union of the two coordinate axes.*

> Troisieme partie.
> 
> 1. Let $a$, $b$, $c$ be three real numbers such that $a(b-c)\neq 0$, and $A$, $B$, $C$ the points of respective coordinates $(0,a)$, $(b,0)$, $(c,0)$. Compute the coordinates of the orthocentre $D$ of triangle $ABC$.
> 
> 2. Let $X$ be the set obtained by taking the union of a line $\Delta$ and a point $M$ not belonging to $\Delta$. Determine $\mathcal{H}(X)$. Show that $\mathcal{H}(X)\cup X$ is an orthocentric set.
> 
> 3. Let $X$ be an orthocentric set contained in the union of the axes $(O,\vec{u})$ and $(O,\vec{v})$ and containing at least three points of $(O,\vec{u})$ distinct from $O$.
>    a. Show that $X$ contains at least three points of $(O,\vec{u})$ with nonzero abscissae of the same sign.
>    b. Show that $X$ contains at least three points of $(O,\vec{u})$ with strictly positive abscissae.
> 
> 4.
>    a. Determine the finite orthocentric sets, containing at most five points and contained in the union of the axes $(O,\vec{u})$ and $(O,\vec{v})$.
>    b. Let $X$ be an orthocentric set contained in the union of the axes $(O,\vec{u})$ and $(O,\vec{v})$ and containing at least six points. Show that there exist two sequences $(x_n)$ and $(x'_n)$ of nonzero reals such that, for every integer $n$, the points of coordinates $(x_n,0)$ and $(x'_n,0)$ belong to $X$, and such that:
> $$\lim_{n\to+\infty} x_n=+\infty,\qquad \lim_{n\to+\infty} x'_n=0.$$
> Can an orthocentric set contained in the union of the axes $(O,\vec{u})$ and $(O,\vec{v})$ and containing at least six points be finite?

*Terza parte: coordinate dell'ortocentro di un triangolo con date coordinate; insieme ortocentrico ottenuto da una linea più un punto esterno; e proprietà di un insieme ortocentrico contenuto nell'unione dei due assi di coordinate.*

*Fourth part: construction of remarkable orthocentric sets on the hyperbola $xy=k$; orthocentre conditions for points on the hyperbola; a quadratic with irrational roots and a similitude $z\mapsto(1-ri)z$; and that the integer-coordinate points of $X$ form an infinite orthocentric set.*

> Quatrieme partie.
> 
> The objective of this part is the construction of remarkable orthocentric sets.
> 
> 1. Let $k$ be a nonzero real and let $Y$ be the hyperbola of equation $xy=k$.
>    a. Let $A$, $B$, $C$, $D$ be four distinct points of $Y$, with respective abscissae $a$, $b$, $c$, $d$. Show that $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{CD}$ are orthogonal if and only if $abcd=-k^2$.
>    b. Let $A$, $B$, $C$ be three distinct points of $Y$, with respective abscissae $a$, $b$, $c$. Determine the orthocentre of $ABC$.
>    c. Show that $Y$ is orthocentric.
> 
> In the whole of the remainder of the fourth part, one considers a nonzero relative integer $q$ and one denotes by $X$ the set of points of equation $x^2+qxy-y^2=1$.
> 
> 2.
>    a. Show that the equation $t^2-qt-1$ has two distinct real roots. Show that these roots are irrational.
> 
> In the whole of the remainder of the fourth part, one denotes by $r$ and $r'$ these two roots and by $s$ the similitude defined by the complex representation $z\mapsto(1-ri)z$.
>    b. Show that $s(X)$ is a hyperbola, of equation $xy=k$, where $k$ is a real number to be determined. Deduce that $X$ is an orthocentric set.
> 
> 3. Let $G$ be the set of points of $X$ with integer coordinates and $\Gamma$ the set of abscissae of the elements of $s(G)$.
>    a. Verify that $\Gamma$ is the set of real numbers of the form $x+ry$, where $x$ and $y$ are two integers such that $(x+ry)(x+r'y)=1$.
>    b. Show that $-1\in\Gamma$; show that $r^2\in\Gamma$.
>    c. Show that the product of two elements of $\Gamma$ is an element of $\Gamma$ and that the inverse of an element of $\Gamma$ is an element of $\Gamma$. Show that $\Gamma$ possesses infinitely many elements.
> 
> 4. Deduce from the above that the set $G$ of integer-coordinate points of $X$ is an infinite orthocentric set.

*Fifth part: an iteration $X_{n+1}=\mathcal{H}(X_n)$ starting from four points in general position, using a similitude result relating the hyperbola $xy=1$ and the degenerate locus $xy=0$; show the minimal period $m$ is $1$ or $2$ and characterize the initial sets achieving each.*

> Cinquieme partie.
> 
> Denote by $Y_1$ the hyperbola of equation $xy=1$ and by $Y_0$ the set of equation $xy=0$, that is the union of the axes $(O,\vec{u})$ and $(O,\vec{v})$.
> 
> One admits the following result:
> "Given four points $A$, $B$, $C$ and $D$ of the plane, there exists a similitude $s$ such that $s(A)$, $s(B)$, $s(C)$ and $s(D)$ all belong to $Y_1$, or else all belong to $Y_0$."
> 
> Let $A_0$, $B_0$, $C_0$ and $D_0$ be four points, three by three non-collinear, and let $X_0=\{A_0,B_0,C_0,D_0\}$. One defines by recurrence $X_{n+1}=\mathcal{H}(X_n)$ for every natural integer $n$. One supposes that there exists a strictly positive integer $n$ such that $X_n=X_0$ and one denotes by $m$ the smallest integer having this property.
> 
> 1. Show that $m=1$ or $m=2$.
> 
> 2. Determine the sets $X_0$ such that $m=1$, then those such that $m=2$.

*Quinta parte: una iterazione $X_{n+1}=\mathcal{H}(X_n)$ partendo da quattro punti in posizione generale, utilizzando un risultato di somiglianza che relaziona l'iperbola $xy=1$ e il locus degenerato $xy=0$; mostrare il periodo minimo $m$ è $1$ o $2$ e caratterizzare gli insiemi iniziali che raggiungono ciascuno di essi.*
