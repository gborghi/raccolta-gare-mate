*Angle bisector, circumcircle tangent, circumcircle intersection angle equality*

> Let $ABC$ be an acute triangle, and let $M$ be the midpoint of side $BC$. Let $\ell$ be the angle bisector of $\angle BAC$, and let $D$ be the intersection of $\ell$ with the tangent to the circumcircle of triangle $ABC$ at $C$. Let $H$ be a point on $\ell$ such that $\angle ABH = 90^\circ$. Let $P$ be the second intersection point (other than $C$) of the circumcircle of triangle $CDH$ and the circumcircle of triangle $ABC$. Prove that $\angle CPM = \angle ACB$.

*Find minimum n for set with exactly 2015 good partitions*

> Let $A$ be a set consisting of $n$ distinct positive integers. A pair of subsets $(A_1, A_2)$ is called a **good partition** if it satisfies the following conditions:
> - $A_1 \neq \emptyset$, $A_2 \neq \emptyset$.
> - $A_1 \cap A_2 = \emptyset$, $A_1 \cup A_2 = A$.
> - The least common multiple of all elements of $A_1$ equals the greatest common divisor of all elements of $A_2$.
> 
> Find the smallest value of $n$ such that there exists a set $A$ with exactly $2015$ good partitions. (Here $\emptyset$ denotes the empty set.)

Una coppia di sottogruppi $(A_1, A_2)$ è chiamata **buona partizione ** se soddisfa le seguenti condizioni: - $A_1 \neq \emptyset$, $A_2 \neq \emptyset$. - $A_1 \cap A_2 = \emptyset$, $A_1 \cup A_2 = A$. - Il più piccolo multiplo comune di tutti gli elementi di $A_1$ è uguale al più grande divisore comune di tutti gli elementi di $A_2$.
> 
> Trova il valore più piccolo di $n$ in modo che esista un insieme $A$ con esattamente $2015$ buone partizioni. (Qui $\emptyset$ indica l'insieme vuoto.)

*Twin polynomials: existence for degree n+1, nonexistence for degree n*

> Let $n$ be an integer with $n \ge 2$. Two real-coefficient polynomials $P(x)$ and $Q(x)$ are called **look-alike** if for every integer $i$ with $1 \le i \le n$, the sequence $P(2015i),\, P(2015i-1),\, \ldots,\, P(2015i - 2014)$ is a rearrangement of $Q(2015i),\, Q(2015i-1),\, \ldots,\, Q(2015i-2014)$.
> 
> (a) Prove that there exist two distinct polynomials of degree $n+1$ that are look-alike.
