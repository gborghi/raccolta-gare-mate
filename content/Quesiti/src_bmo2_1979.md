*Prove inequality for four different positive reals or find counterexample*

> $a, b, c, d$ are different positive real numbers. Prove that if at least one of the numbers $c$ and $d$ lies between the numbers $a$ and $b$, or at least one of the numbers $a$ and $b$ lies between the numbers $c$ and $d$, then
> $$(*) \quad \sqrt{(a+b)(c+d)} \ge \sqrt{ab} + \sqrt{cd}.$$
> Otherwise show that the four numbers can be chosen so that $(*)$ is false.

*Prove la disuguaglianza per quattro differenti valori positivi o trova il contromodo*

> $a, b, c, d$ sono diversi numeri reali positivi. Prova che se almeno uno dei numeri $c$ e $d$ si trova tra i numeri $a$ e $b$, o almeno uno dei numeri $a$ e $b$ si trova tra i numeri $c$ e $d$, allora $$(*) \quad \sqrt{(a+b)(c+d)} \ge \sqrt{ab} + \sqrt{cd}.$$ dimostra altrimenti che i quattro numeri possono essere scelti in modo tale che $(*)$ sia falso.

*Two equilateral triangles share vertex C; prove triangles O'M and OA'B' similar*

> Two equilateral triangles have a common vertex $C$. Going round each triangle in the anti-clockwise direction the vertices are lettered $C, A, B$ and $C, A', B'$. $O$ is the centre of triangle $CAB$ and neither $A'$ nor $B'$ coincides with $O$. $M$ is the midpoint of $A'B'$ and $N$ is the midpoint of $AB'$. Prove that triangles $O'MN$ and $OA'B'$ are similar.

*Sequence defined by floor sums; determine a_1979*

> The sequence of positive integers $a_n$ is defined by
> $$a_0 = 1979, \quad a_{n+1} = \lfloor \tfrac{1}{2}(a_0 + a_1 + a_2 + \cdots + a_n) \rfloor \quad (n \ge 0),$$
> where $\lfloor x \rfloor$ denotes the greatest integer not greater than $x$. (For example $\lfloor 3 \rfloor = 3$ and $\lfloor 5 \rfloor = 5$.) Determine $a_{1979}$.

*Binary digit sum identity for all positive integers*

> Let $b(k)$ denote the number of $1$'s in the binary expansion of the non-negative integer $k$. For example $b(13) = 3$ since $13$ is $1101$ in binary. Prove that for all positive integers $n$,
> $$\sum_{k=0}^{2^n - 1} (-1)^{b(k)} k^n = (-1)^n \cdot 2^{\frac{1}{2}n(n-1)}.$$

*Identificazione della somma binaria dei numeri interi positivi*

> $b(k)$ indichi il numero di $1$ nell'espansione binaria del numero intero non negativo $k$. Ad esempio $b(13) = 3$ dal momento che $13$ è $1101$ in binario. Prova che per tutti gli integri positivi $n$, $$\sum_{k=0}^{2^n - 1} (-1)^{b(k)} k^n = (-1)^n \cdot 2^{\frac{1}{2}n(n-1)}.$$
