*Differenza massima tra due angoli di triangolo scaleno intero*

> Un triangolo scaleno e con area diversa da zero ha tutti gli angoli con misura un numero intero positivo di

> B) 179 
> C) 178 
> D) 177 
> E) 176 
> La risposta corretta è la E 
> Con le condizioni descritte non si può avere un angolo di misura 0°. Dette 𝛼< 𝛽< 𝛾 le misure in gradi dei tre 
> angoli, risulta 𝛾= 180 −𝛼−𝛽 da cui la differenza fra il maggiore e il minore risulta uguale a 𝛾−𝛼= 180 −

*Maximum difference between two angles of an integer-sided scalene triangle*

> A scalene triangle with non-zero area has all angles measuring a positive integer number of degrees. What is the maximum possible difference, in degrees, between two angles?
> A) 175 
> B) 179 
> C) 178 
> D) 177 
> E) 176 
> The correct answer is E. With the conditions described, an angle of measure 0° cannot occur. Letting 𝛼 < 𝛽 < 𝛾 be the measures in degrees of the three angles, we have 𝛾 = 180 − 𝛼 − 𝛽, from which the difference between the largest and the smallest angle is 𝛾 − 𝛼 = 180 − 2𝛼 − 𝛽; therefore, it is advisable to take 𝛼 as small as possible (𝛼 = 1) and similarly to take 𝛽 as small as possible (𝛽 = 2, since if 𝛽 = 1 the triangle would be isosceles). It follows that 𝛾 − 𝛼 = 180 − 2 − 2 = 176.

*Numero massimo di cavalieri sull'isola di furfanti e cavalieri*

> Sull’isola dei furfanti (che mentono sempre) e dei cavalieri (che dicono sempre il vero) vivono 10 persone. 
> Cinque di queste stanno zitte mentre ognuna delle altre cinque fa un’affermazione: “Qui siamo tutti furfanti!”; 
> “Qui siamo tutti cavalieri!”; “Almeno 4 di noi sono furfanti”; “Esattamente 6 di noi sono furfanti”; “Al massimo 
> 5 di noi sono cavalieri”. Quanti sono al massimo i cavalieri nell’isola? 
> A) 5 
> B) 4 
> C) 6 
> D) 8 
> E) 7 
> La risposta corretta è la C 
> Analizziamo ciascuna affermazione. La persona che afferma “Qui siamo tutti furfanti” non può essere un 
> cavaliere (altrimenti direbbe il vero, e quindi le 10 persone sarebbero tutte furfanti, contro l’ipotesi che lui è un 
> cavaliere) e quindi è un furfante. D’altro canto, anche la seconda persona è un furfante, perché non è vero che 
> sono tutti cavalieri (abbiamo appena osservato che la prima persona che parla è un furfante). Se la terza persona 
> fosse un furfante, i furfanti sarebbero al più 3, ma allora la persona successiva dovrebbe essere un cavaliere e dire 
> la verità (ci sono esattamente 6 furfanti), mentre abbiamo appena detto che i furfanti sono al più 3. Dunque la 
> terza persona deve essere un cavaliere: pertanto, ci sono almeno 4 furfanti e, di conseguenza, il numero dei 
> cavalieri è al massimo 6. La presenza di 4 furfanti e 6 cavalieri è compatibile con le ultime due affermazioni: in 
> particolare, se gli ultimi due sono furfanti, ci sono esattamente 4 furfanti e 6 cavalieri (la terza persona e tutti 
> quelli che non hanno parlato).

*Maximum number of knights on the Island of Rogues and Knights*

> On the island of scoundrels (who always lie) and knights (who always tell the truth), 10 people live. Five of them remain silent, while each of the other five makes a statement: "We are all scoundrels here!"; "We are all knights here!"; "At least 4 of us are scoundrels"; "Exactly 6 of us are scoundrels"; "At most 5 of us are knights". What is the maximum number of knights on the island?
> A) 5 
> B) 4 
> C) 6 
> D) 8 
> E) 7 
> The correct answer is C. Let's examine each statement. The person who says, "Here we are all scoundrels," cannot be a knight (otherwise they would be telling the truth, and so all 10 people would be scoundrels, contrary to the assumption that they are a knight), and therefore is a scoundrel. On the other hand, the second person is also a scoundrel, because it is not true that they are all knights (we have just observed that the first person to speak is a scoundrel). If the third person were a scoundrel, there would be at most 3 scoundrels, but then the next person would have to be a knight and tell the truth (there are exactly 6 scoundrels), whereas we have just said that there are at most 3 scoundrels. Therefore, the third person must be a knight: hence, there are at least 4 scoundrels and, consequently, the number of knights is at most 6. The presence of 4 scoundrels and 6 knights is consistent with the last two statements: in particular, if the last two are scoundrels, there are exactly 4 scoundrels and 6 knights (the third person and all those who did not speak).

*Maximum n for a polygon with angles in an arithmetic progression with a common difference of 30*

> Simone arranges in increasing order the measures of the interior angles of a convex polygon with 𝑛 sides (where 𝑛 > 2), and observes that each angle, starting from the second, is 30° greater than the preceding one. What is the maximum possible value of 𝑛?
> A) 5 
> B) 3 
> C) 4 
> D) 6 
> E) 8 
> The correct answer is A. Let x > 0 be the measure, in degrees, of the smallest angle, so that the other angles measure, in order, x + 30°, x + 60°, x + 90°, x + 120°, and x + 150°. It is certainly impossible to go any further, since already x + 180° > 180° and the polygon would no longer be convex. So for now we rule out n > 6. It is observed that with n = 6 it is impossible to construct a polygon that also satisfies the condition on the sum of the angles, S = (6 − 2) · 180° = 720°. Indeed, x + (x + 30°) + (x + 60°) + (x + 90°) + (x + 120°) + (x + 150°) = 6x + 450° = 720°, whence x = 45°. However, in this case the last angle measures 45° + 150° = 195° > 180°, so n = 6 does not work. Trying n = 5 gives 5x + 300° = 540°, whence x = 48°, and the largest angle is 48° + 120° = 168° < 180°; therefore n = 5 works and is the solution.

*Unordered pairs of balls of different colors*

> Claudia has 11 balls, all of which are different from one another and are colored as follows: 4 red balls, 2 green balls, and 5 blue balls. In how many ways can she choose an unordered pair of balls of different colors?
> A) 3 
> B) 38 
> C) 40 
> D) 45 
> E) 76 
> The correct answer is B. The valid pairs are: red-green, red-blue, and green-blue. We must not also count the three pairs green-red, blue-red, and blue-green, since that would mean counting ordered pairs. Since all the balls are different, we can choose the red ball in 4 ways, the green ball in 2 ways, and the blue ball in 5 ways. By the fundamental principle of combinatorial calculus (or by the Cartesian product rule), the number of pairs is found by multiplying the cardinalities of the starting sets, namely 4 ∙ 2 = 8, 4 ∙ 5 = 20, and 2 ∙ 5 = 10. These three quantities must be added because the three situations are independent of one another; therefore, the solution is 8 + 20 + 10 = 38.
