*Partition lattice points with at least one coordinate not divisible by 4 into unit-distance pairs.*

> In a coordinate plane, consider the set of points with integer coordinates at least one of which is not divisible by 4. Prove that these points can be partitioned into pairs such that the distance between points in each pair equals 1.
> 
> In other words, an infinite chessboard, whose cells with both coordinates divisible by 4 are cut out, cannot be tiled by dominoes.

*Partiziona i punti del reticolo con almeno una coordinata non divisibile per 4 in coppie a distanza unitaria.*

> In un piano cartesiano, si consideri l'insieme dei punti a coordinate intere, almeno una delle quali non è divisibile per 4. Dimostrare che questi punti possono essere suddivisi in coppie tali che la distanza tra i punti di ciascuna coppia sia uguale a 1.

> In altre parole, una scacchiera infinita, in cui sono state rimosse le caselle le cui due coordinate sono entrambe divisibili per 4, non può essere coperta con i domino.

> Prove that if $m$ is a natural number and $P,Q,R$ polynomials of degrees less than $m$ satisfying

> then each of the polynomials is zero.

*For the recursively defined sequence, prove it contains infinitely many nonpositive terms.*

> For a given real number $a$, define the sequence $(a_n)$ by $a_1=a$ and
> $$a_{n+1}=\begin{cases} \frac{1}{2}\left(a_n-\frac{1}{a_n}\right) & \text{if } a_n\ne 0, \\ 0 & \text{if } a_n=0. \end{cases}$$
> Prove that the sequence $(a_n)$ contains infinitely many nonpositive terms.

**Topic:** [[topic_algebra|Algebra]], [[topic_trigonometria|Trigonometry]], [[topic_funzionali|Functional equations / sequences]]
**Metodo:** [[method_trigonometria|Trigonometria]], [[method_ricorsione|Ricorsione]]

**Fonte:** [apri PDF](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1AGtrG0C8tcQJU36uFC-bh5dGK0KglZ6h/view)

*For a tetrahedron with altitudes h_i and distances d_i between opposite edge pairs, prove the reciprocal-square sums are equal.*

> Prove that if $h_1,h_2,h_3,h_4$ are the altitudes of a tetrahedron and $d_1,d_2,d_3$ are the distances between the pairs of opposite edges of the tetrahedron, then
> $$\frac{1}{h_1^2}+\frac{1}{h_2^2}+\frac{1}{h_3^2}+\frac{1}{h_4^2}=\frac{1}{d_1^2}+\frac{1}{d_2^2}+\frac{1}{d_3^2}.$$

**Metodo:** [[method_coordinate|Coordinate]]

> $$\frac{1}{h_1^2}+\frac{1}{h_2^2}+\frac{1}{h_3^2}+\frac{1}{h_4^2}=\frac{1}{d_1^2}+\frac{1}{d_2^2}+\frac{1}{d_3^2}.$$
