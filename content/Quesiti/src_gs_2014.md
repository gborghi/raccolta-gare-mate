*The inexplicable case of the tourist*

![[src_gs_2014__prob1.png]]

> The inexplicable case of the tourist (20 points)
> At MISTERIUS we tell of events that remain unexplained. A tourist, from the country that uses the ctrome as its unit of length and the loki as its unit of weight, travels to a country where lengths are measured in cmotre and weights in koli. The tourist knows that 15 ctrome correspond to 7 cmotre, while 40 loki are 3 koli. He also knows that 14 cm correspond to 3 ctrome, while 2 kg are 9 koli. Incredibly, at the border, the tourist declares that he can calculate the weight in loki of 20 cmotre^3 of water. But what weight in loki does the tourist calculate? [Recall that 1 dm^3 of water weighs 1 kg.]

*Probability at the bar*

> Probability at the bar (40 points)
> MISTERIUS has managed to recover the video of the famous dice game between Bagolot and Enrico Fermi at the bar in Göttingen in 1923. As can be seen in the video, Bagolot and Fermi have three dice: one with 4 faces, numbered from 1 to 4; another with 6 faces, numbered from 1 to 6; the last one with 8 faces, numbered from 1 to 8. Bagolot challenges Fermi to the following gamble: Fermi rolls all the dice once; then Bagolot throws one of them away; Fermi rolls the two remaining dice a second time. All the points obtained are added up (from the first roll of three dice and the second roll of the two remaining ones): if the sum is 15, Fermi wins 100 marks from Bagolot; otherwise Bagolot wins 10 from Fermi. One sees Fermi roll all three dice together once, getting 8 as the sum of the faces. Unfortunately the video ends abruptly; it is known that at that point something astonishing happened: Bagolot evaluated perfectly his probabilities of winning the 10 marks depending on the die he would throw away. What was his best probability of winning the 10 marks? [Write as the solution the first 4 digits after the decimal point of the result.]

> The fourth millionaire problem (70 points)
> The fourth problem for becoming a millionaire is the following: consider the polynomial 2x^3 − 11x^2 + 4x + 6 and let a, b, c be its roots. Then let D\_k := (a^2(b + c) + b^2(a + c) + c^2(a + b))^k, let k\_1 be the smallest positive integer value of k such that the first digit from the left of D\_k is 7, and k\_2 the smallest positive integer value of k such that the first digit from the left of D\_k is 9. Compute k\_1 × k\_2.

**Answer:** 2438
