*5x5 grid filled like Latin square; analyze score below diagonal*

> A $5\times 5$ square is divided into $25$ unit squares. One of the numbers $1, 2, 3, 4, 5$ is inserted into each of the unit squares in such a way that each row, each column and each of the two diagonals contains each of the five numbers once and only once. The sum of the numbers in the four squares immediately below the diagonal from top left to bottom right is called the \emph{score}.
> Show that it is impossible for the score to be $20$.
> What is the highest possible score?

**Metodo:** [[method_casework|Casework]], [[method_simmetria|Simmetria]]

<div class="qlang-split" data-lang="it"></div>


*Griglia 5x5 riempita come un quadrato latino; analizza il punteggio sotto la diagonale*

> Un quadrato $5\times 5$ è diviso in $25$ quadratini unitari. Uno dei numeri $1, 2, 3, 4, 5$ viene inserito in ciascuno dei quadratini unitari in modo che ogni riga, ogni colonna e ciascuna delle due diagonali contenga ciascuno dei cinque numeri una sola volta e una volta sola. La somma dei numeri nei quattro quadratini immediatamente sotto la diagonale che va dall'angolo in alto a sinistra a quello in basso a destra è chiamata \emph{punteggio}. Dimostrare che è impossibile che il punteggio sia $20$. Qual è il punteggio più alto possibile?

*Mod-100 Fibonacci-like sequence; sum of squares mod 8*

> Let $a_1 = 19$, $a_2 = 98$. For $n \ge 1$, define $a_{n+2}$ to be the remainder of $a_n + a_{n+1}$ when it is divided by $100$. What is the remainder when
> $$a_1^2 + a_2^2 + \cdots + a_{1998}^2$$
> is divided by $8$?

*Isosceles triangle, parallelogram; prove M is midpoint of DA*

> $ABP$ is an isosceles triangle with $AB = AP$ and $\angle PAB$ acute. $PC$ is the line through $P$ perpendicular to $BP$, and $C$ is a point on this line on the same side of $BP$ as $A$. (You may assume that $C$ is not on the line $AB$.) $D$ completes the parallelogram $ABCD$. $PC$ meets $DA$ at $M$.
> Prove that $M$ is the midpoint of $DA$.

*Show unique positive-integer sequence satisfies given recurrence*

> Show that there is a unique sequence of positive integers $(a_n)$ satisfying the following conditions:
> $$a_1 = 1, \quad a_2 = 2, \quad a_4 = 12,$$
> $$a_{n+1}a_{n-1} = a_n^2 \pm 1 \quad \text{for} \quad n = 2, 3, 4, \ldots.$$
