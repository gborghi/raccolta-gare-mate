*Prove a product-sum identity for a recursively defined sequence*

> A sequence of real numbers $a_1, a_2, \ldots$ is defined by $a_1 \neq 0, 1$, $a_2 = 1 - a_1$, $a_{n+1} = 1 - a_n(a_{n-1} \cdots a_1)$, $n = 1, 2, \ldots$. Prove that for any positive integer $n$, $a_1 a_2 \cdots a_n \left(\frac{1}{a_1} + \frac{1}{a_1 a_2} + \cdots + \frac{1}{a_1 a_2 \cdots a_n}\right) = 1.$ (Posed by Li Shenghong)

*In an isosceles triangle with inscribed circle, prove a concyclicity and a ratio relation involving points built from the incircle*

> In $\triangle ABC$, $AB = AC$, the inscribed circle $I$ touches $BC$, $CA$ and $AB$ at points $D$, $E$ and $F$ respectively. $P$ is a point on arc $\widehat{EF}$ (not containing $D$). Line $BP$ intersects the circle $I$ at another point $Q$, and lines $EP$, $EQ$ meet line $BC$ at $M$, $N$ respectively. Prove that
> (1) $P$, $F$, $B$, $M$ are concyclic.
> (2) $\frac{EM}{EN} = \frac{BD}{BP}$.
> (Posed by Bian Hongping)

*Determine boundedness conditions for a sequence defined by x_{n+1}=a x_n^n + b*

> Given an integer $n \geqslant 2$, and two real numbers $a$, $b$ with $a > 0$ and $b \neq 0$, the sequence $\{x_n\}$ is such that $x_1 = b$ and $x_{n+1} = ax_n^n + b$, $n = 1, 2, \ldots$. Prove that:
> (1) When $b < 0$ and $n$ is even, the sequence $\{x_n\}$ is bounded if and only if $ab^{n-1} \geqslant 2$;
> (2) When $b < 0$ and $n$ is odd, or when $b > 0$, the sequence $\{x_n\}$ is bounded if and only if $ab^{n-1} \leqslant \frac{(n-1)^{n-1}}{n^n}$.
> (Posed by Zhu Huawei and Fu Yunhao)

*Determinare le condizioni di limitatezza per una successione definita da x_{n+1}=a x_n^n + b*

> Dato un intero $n \geqslant 2$ e due numeri reali $a$, $b$ con $a > 0$ e $b \neq 0$, la successione $\{x_n\}$ è tale che $x_1 = b$ e $x_{n+1} = ax_n^n + b$, $n = 1, 2, \ldots$. Dimostrare che: (1) Quando $b < 0$ e $n$ è pari, la successione $\{x_n\}$ è limitata se e solo se $ab^{n-1} \geqslant 2$; (2) Quando $b < 0$ e $n$ è dispari, oppure quando $b > 0$, la successione $\{x_n\}$ è limitata se e solo se $ab^{n-1} \leqslant \frac{(n-1)^{n-1}}{n^n}$. (Proposto da Zhu Huawei e Fu Yunhao)

*Four frogs on a line jump by reflection over another frog; prove they cannot reach equal neighbor distances of 2008*

> Four frogs are positioned at four points on a straight line so that the distances between any two neighboring frogs are all equal to one unit of length. A frog may jump and reach a new point that is the reflection of its original position, by taking any one of the other three frogs as the reference point. Prove that there is no case where the distances between any two neighboring points, where the frogs stay, will equal to $2008$ units of length. (Posed by Liu Shixiong)
