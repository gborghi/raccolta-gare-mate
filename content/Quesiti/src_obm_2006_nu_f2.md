*Prove integral inequality for increasing integrable function*

> Let $f : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ be an integrable and increasing function. Prove that

*Count 2x2 integer matrices with determinant kn+1 mod n*

> Prove that, for every integer $n \ge 2$, the number of $2 \times 2$ matrices with integer entries belonging to the set $\{0, 1, 2, \ldots, n-1\}$ whose determinant has the form $kn + 1$ for some integer $k$ is equal to
> $$n^3 \cdot \prod_{\substack{p \text{ prime} \\ p \mid n}} \left(1 - \frac{1}{p^2}\right).$$

*Billiard on elliptic table: prove ball returns to B after A-B-C-A path*

> A billiard table has the shape of an ellipse and has no pockets. When a ball hits a point $P$ on the edge of the table, it follows a direction symmetric with respect to the normal to the ellipse at $P$. Prove that if a ball starts from a point $A$ on the ellipse and, after bouncing at points $B$ and $C$, returns to $A$, then it will bounce at $B$ again.

*Billiard su tavola ellittica: prova che la palla ritorna a B dopo il percorso A-B-C-A*

> Un tavolo da biliardo ha la forma di un'ellisse e non ha tasche. Quando una palla colpisce un punto $P$ sul bordo della tavola, segue una direzione simmetrica rispetto alla normale all'ellisse a $P$. Prova che se una palla parte da un punto $A$ sull'ellisse e, dopo essere rimbalzata ai punti $B$ e $C$, ritorna a $A$, rimbalzerà di nuovo a $B$.

*Irreducible polynomial over Q with product-1 roots has even degree*

> Let $p$ be an irreducible polynomial in $\mathbb{Q}[x]$ with rational coefficients and degree greater than $1$. Prove that if $p$ has two roots $r$ and $s$ whose product is $1$ (i.e., $rs = 1$), then the degree of $p$ is even.

*Series 1/f(n) converges iff series f^{-1}(n)/n^2 converges*

> Let $f : [0, +\infty) \to [0, +\infty)$ be an increasing and bijective function. Prove that the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{f(n)}$ converges if and only if the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{f^{-1}(n)}{n^2}$ converges, where $f^{-1}$ denotes the inverse function of $f$.

*No integer alternating product of A^a_i B^b_i equals identity for n>1*

> Consider the matrices
> $$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \quad \text{and} \quad B = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}.$$
> Prove that, for $n > 1$, there do not exist integers $a_1, a_2, a_3, \ldots, a_n$ and $b_1, b_2, \ldots, b_{n-1}, b_n$ with $a_2, a_3, \ldots, a_n$ and $b_1, b_2, \ldots, b_{n-1}$ all nonzero, such that
> $$A^{a_1} \cdot B^{b_1} \cdot A^{a_2} \cdot B^{b_2} \cdots A^{a_n} \cdot B^{b_n} = I,$$
> where $I$ is the $2 \times 2$ identity matrix.

**Topic:** [[topic_algebra|Algebra]], [[topic_combinatoria|Combinatoria]]
**Metodo:** [[method_invarianti|Invarianti]], [[method_induzione|Induzione]]
**Abilita:** [[skill_astrazione|Astrazione]], [[skill_manipolazione_algebrica|Manipolazione algebrica]], [[skill_ragionamento_geometrico|Ragionamento geometrico]]
**Area:** [[Algebra e Analisi]], [[Combinatoria, Logica e Probabilita]]
**Fonte:** [apri PDF](https://drive.google.com/file/d/18lHsUkA6TjiVCKvRGHHFZ98nGkOcKHvM/view)

*Nessun prodotto intero alternativo di A^a_i B^b_i è uguale all'identità per n>1*

> Considera le matrici $$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \quad \text{and} \quad B = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}.$$ Prove che, per $n > 1$, non esistono numeri interi $a_1, a_2, a_3, \ldots, a_n$ e $b_1, b_2, \ldots, b_{n-1}, b_n$ con $a_2, a_3, \ldots, a_n$ e $b_1, b_2, \ldots, b_{n-1}$ tutti non zero, in modo tale che $$A^{a_1} \cdot B^{b_1} \cdot A^{a_2} \cdot B^{b_2} \cdots A^{a_n} \cdot B^{b_n} = I,$$ dove $I$ è la matrice di identità $2 \times 2$.
