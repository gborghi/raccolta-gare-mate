*Right triangle ABC with a point O (AO=BO=CO); prove angle ACB = 30 degrees.*

> (Part B, Problem 1.) Let $ABC$ be a right triangle, and let $O$ be a point with $AO=BO=CO$ (so $O$ is the midpoint of the hypotenuse / circumcenter). A point $I$ satisfies $\angle BAI=\angle OAI=45^\circ$. Since triangle $ABC$ is right-angled, one has $AO=BO=CO$, and from $\angle BAI=\angle OAI=45^\circ$ together with the common side $AI$ the triangles $ABI$ and $AOI$ are congruent (ASA), giving $AB=BO$; with $AB=BO=AO$ the triangle $ABO$ is equilateral. Conclude the measure of $\angle ACB$. [Source is the solutions sheet; statement reconstructed from the solution, which concludes $\angle ACB=30^\circ$.]

*Sum of reciprocals of three quadratic values built from roots alpha,beta equals 0.*

> (Part B, Problem 2.) One has the identity $(x-2)(x-3)+(x-3)(x+1)+(x+1)(x-2)=3(x-\alpha)(x-\beta)$, where $\alpha,\beta$ are its roots. Substituting $x=-1,2,3$ gives $(\alpha+1)(\beta+1)=4$, $(\alpha-2)(\beta-2)=-1$ and $(\alpha-3)(\beta-3)=\dfrac{4}{3}$. Compute $\dfrac{1}{(\alpha+1)(\beta+1)}+\dfrac{1}{(\alpha-2)(\beta-2)}+\dfrac{1}{(\alpha-3)(\beta-3)}$. [Source is the solutions sheet; statement reconstructed from the solution, which yields $\frac{1}{4}-1+\frac{3}{4}=0$.]

*Count divisors of 23^5-23 and prove n^3-n is always a multiple of 30.*

> (Part B, Problem 3.) a) Let $N=23^5-23=23\cdot(23^4-1)=23\cdot(23^2+1)(23^2-1)=23\cdot(23^2+1)(23+1)(23-1)=23\cdot530\cdot24\cdot22=2^5\cdot3\cdot5\cdot11\cdot23\cdot53$. Determine the number of positive divisors of $N$ (which is $6\times2\times2\times2\times2=192$). b) Let $N=n^3-n=n(n-1)(n+1)$. Show that $N$ is always a multiple of $30$. (Among the three consecutive integers $n-1,n,n+1$ one is even and one is a multiple of $3$; if none is a multiple of $5$ then $n\equiv2$ or $3\pmod5$, and $n^2+1$ is a multiple of $5$, so $n^3-n$ is a multiple of $5$. Hence $N$ is a multiple of $2\cdot3\cdot5=30$.) [Source is the solutions sheet; statement reconstructed from the solution. Part-a OCR dropped a prime factor; the divisor count 192 implies $N=2^5\cdot3\cdot5\cdot11\cdot23\cdot53$.]

*Count valid A/V colorings of a board with no two equal in adjacent row/column cells.*

> (Part B, Problem 4.) Each cell of an $n\times n$ board is to be colored with one of the letters $A$ or $V$, so that no two horizontally or vertically consecutive cells carry the same letter (each row and each column is an alternating sequence). Once the first row is fixed, the rest of the board is determined: wherever two consecutive equal letters appear in a row, the two cells immediately below them are forced to the other letter, and so on. Determine the number of valid colorings. [Source is the solutions sheet; statement reconstructed from the solution. The general count is $2^{n+1}-2$ in one case plus $2^{n+1}$ in the other; for the specific board of the problem the answer is $30+32=62$.]
