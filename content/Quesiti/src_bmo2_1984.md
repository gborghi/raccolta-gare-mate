*Tetrahedron ABCD with equal edges; plane through midpoints minimises quadrilateral area*

> $ABCD$ is a tetrahedron with $DA = DB = DC = d$ and $AB = BC = CA = e$. $M$ and $N$ are the midpoints of $AB$ and $CD$. A plane $\pi$ passes through $MN$ and cuts $AD$ and $BC$ at $P$ and $Q$ respectively.
> 
> (i) Prove that $AP/AD = BQ/BC$ ($ = t$, say).
> 
> (ii) Determine with proof that value of $t$, expressed in terms of $d$ and $e$, which minimises the area of the quadrilateral $MQNP$.

*Count full expressions for n as sums of powers of 2; prove recurrence*

> Let $b_n$ be the number of ways of expressing the positive integer $n$ as a sum of one or more, not necessarily distinct, powers of $2$; here $1$ ($= 2^0$) is regarded as a power of $2$. Order of the summands is immaterial, so for instance $b_4 = 4$, the expressions in question being
> $$1+1+1+1, \quad 1+1+2, \quad 2+2, \quad 4.$$
> Call such an expression $full$ if it includes at least one summand $2^i$ for $0 \le i \le k$, where $2^k$ is the largest summand occurring in it. For example the first two of the above expressions for $4$ are full, the others are not. Let $c_n$ be the number of full expressions for $n$. Prove that
> $$c_{n+1} = 2c_n$$
> for $m \ge 1$.

*Dimostrare che $c_{n+1} = 2c_n$ per $m \ge 1$*

> Sia $b_n$ il numero di modi di esprimere l'intero positivo $n$ come somma di una o più potenze di $2$, non necessariamente distinte; qui $1$ ($= 2^0$) si considera una potenza di $2$. L'ordine degli addendi è irrilevante, così per esempio $b_4 = 4$, e le espressioni in questione sono
> $$1+1+1+1, \quad 1+1+2, \quad 2+2, \quad 4.$$
> Si chiami $full$ una tale espressione se include almeno un addendo $2^i$ per $0 \le i \le k$, dove $2^k$ è l'addendo più grande che vi compare. Per esempio le prime due delle espressioni precedenti per $4$ sono full, le altre no. Sia $c_n$ il numero di espressioni full per $n$. Dimostrare che
> $$c_{n+1} = 2c_n$$
> per $m \ge 1$.

*Polynomial approximation to p/q on interval of length 1/q*

> Let $p$ and $q$ be positive integers. Show that there exists an interval $I$ of length $\frac{1}{q}$ and a polynomial $P$ with integer coefficients such that, for all $x$ in $I$,
> $$\left| P(x) - \frac{p}{q} \right| \le \frac{1}{q^2}.$$
