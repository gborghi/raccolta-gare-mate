*Logic puzzle: four doors, exactly one of four messages lies*

> Roraima Jonas, an intrepid adventurer, upon leaving a cave comes across four doors, numbered $1$ to $4$, and four messages. The messages say:
> Message 1: "Doors 1 and 2 are safe."
> Message 2: "Exactly two of doors 1, 2 and 3 are safe."
> Message 3: "Door 1 is safe."
> Message 4: "All doors are safe."
> Roraima Jonas is studious and, for this reason, knows that exactly one of the messages lies and the others tell the truth (that is, only one is a lie). However, exactly one of the doors is not safe (it would activate a trap). Which door can Roraima Jonas guarantee to be safe?
> (A) 1 (B) 2 (C) 3 (D) 4 (E) There is no door that Roraima Jonas can guarantee to be safe.

*Minimum number of key presses on a restricted operation-first calculator to reach 100*

> On a very simple calculator it is not possible to press two digits without pressing one of the operations $+$, $-$, $\times$ or $\div$ between the pressed digits. When a digit is pressed, the calculator performs the operation immediately. The calculator starts with $0$ on the display and the first key pressed must be an operation. That is, first one presses an operation, then a digit, then an operation, and so on. For example, one way to make $29$ appear on the display is to press $\boxed{+}$ and then $\boxed{7}$, making $0 + 7 = 7$ appear on the display; then press $\boxed{\times}$ and $\boxed{5}$, obtaining $7 \times 5 = 35$ on the display, and finish by pressing $\boxed{-}$ and $\boxed{6}$, giving as result $35 - 6 = 29$. Thus, it is possible to obtain $29$ with $6$ key presses. Pedro wants the number $100$ to appear on the display. What is the minimum number of key presses, counting operations and digits, that Pedro must make on the calculator?
> (A) $2$ (B) $4$ (C) $6$ (D) $8$ (E) $10$

*Date today under Julian calendar given Gregorian reform of October 1582*

> In Portugal, October $4$, $1582$ was the last day of the Julian calendar, which was replaced by the calendar currently adopted, the Gregorian calendar. The following day was defined as October $15$, $1582$, that is, the days October $5$ to $14$, $1582$ did not exist. The only difference between the calendars is that, in the Julian calendar, all years multiple of $4$ were leap years; in the Gregorian calendar, the years that are multiples of $100$, but not of $400$, are not leap years. Thus, $1900$ would be a leap year in the Julian calendar, but not in the Gregorian calendar. What day would today, June $3$, $2014$, be if we had not changed the calendar?
> (A) May 20, 2014 (B) May 21, 2014 (C) May 22, 2014 (D) June 16, 2014 (E) June 17, 2014

Qual è la probabilità che una moneta con lati $3$ e $4$ atterri totalmente all'interno di una piastrella formata da rettangoli con lati $10$ e $20$? (A) Meno di $0.375$. (B) Esattamente $0.375$. (C) Più di $0.375$ e meno di $0.595$. (D) Esattamente $0.595$. (E) Più di $0.595$.
