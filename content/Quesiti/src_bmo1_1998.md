*5x5 Latin square with diagonals; show sub-diagonal score cannot be 20*

> A $5 \times 5$ square is divided into 25 unit squares. One of the numbers $1, 2, 3, 4, 5$ is inserted into each of the unit squares in such a way that each row, each column and each of the two diagonals contains each of the five numbers once and only once. The sum of the numbers in the four squares immediately below the diagonal from top left to bottom right is called the score.
> 
> Show that it is impossible for the score to be 20.
> 
> What is the highest possible score?

<span class="qlang-split" data-lang="it"></span>


*5x5 quadrato latino con diagonali; mostra il punteggio subdiagonale non può essere 20*

> Un quadrato $5 \times 5$ è diviso in 25 quadrati di unità. Uno dei numeri $1, 2, 3, 4, 5$ viene inserito in ciascun quadrato di unità in modo tale che ogni riga, ogni colonna e ciascuna delle due diagonali contenga ciascuno dei cinque numeri una sola volta. La somma dei numeri nei quattro quadrati immediatamente sotto la diagonale dall'alto a sinistra a basso a destra si chiama punteggio.
> 
> Mostrate che è impossibile che il punteggio sia 20.
> 
> Qual è il punteggio più alto possibile?

*Recurrence mod 100; find remainder of sum of squares mod 8*

> Let $a_0 = 19$, $a_1 = 98$. For $n \ge 1$, define $a_{n+1}$ to be the remainder of $a_n + a_{n-1}$ when divided by 100. What is the remainder when
> $$a_0^2 + a_1^2 + a_2^2 + \cdots + a_{1998}^2$$
> is divided by 8?

*Isosceles triangle ABP; show equal angles and midpoint*

> $ABP$ is an isosceles triangle with $AB = AP$ and $\angle PAB$ acute. $PC$ is the foot of the perpendicular from $P$ to $AB$, and $C$ is a point on the line $BP$ on the $AB$ side. (You may assume that $C$ is not on the line $AB$.) $D$ completes the parallelogram $ABCD$. Show that $\angle APC = \angle ABC$ and that $M$ is the midpoint of $DA$.

*Unique positive-integer sequence; show a term divisible by 87*

> Show that there is a unique sequence of positive integers $(a_n)$ satisfying the following conditions:
> $$a_1 = 1, \quad a_2 = 2, \quad a_{n+2} = a_n^2 + a_{n+1} \quad \text{for } n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$$
> and that $a_{1998}$ is divisible by $87$.
