*Trig inequalities: simultaneous x exists iff sum condition*

> Let $\theta_i \in \left(-\dfrac{\pi}{2}, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$, $i = 1, 2, 3, 4$. Prove that there exists $x \in \mathbb{R}$ such that the two inequalities
> $$\cos^2\theta_1 \cos^2\theta_2 - (\sin\theta_1 \sin\theta_2 - x)^2 \geq 0,$$
> $$\cos^2\theta_3 \cos^2\theta_4 - (\sin\theta_3 \sin\theta_4 - x)^2 \geq 0$$
> hold simultaneously if and only if
> $$\sum_{i=1}^{4} \sin^2\theta_i \leq 2\left(1 + \prod_{i=1}^{4} \sin\theta_i + \prod_{i=1}^{4} \cos\theta_i\right).$$
> (Posed by Li Shenghong)

> A circle intersects sides $BC$, $CA$, $AB$ of $\triangle ABC$ at two points for each side in the following order: $(D_1, D_2)$, $(E_1, E_2)$, $(F_1, F_2)$. *Circular pool frogs: prove equilibrium is reached*

> As seen in Figure 3, a circular pool is divided into $2n$ ($n \geq 5$) "grids". Two grids are said to be neighbors if they have a common side or an arc. When frogs jump simultaneously into the grids (with at least one frog per grid), an "eruption" occurs in a grid if there are at least three frogs in it. After an eruption in a grid, one frog jumps from that grid into each of its neighboring grids. Prove that after finitely many steps, the distribution of frogs will be in equilibrium (i.e., no eruption occurs). (Posed by Su Chun)

<span class="qlang-split" data-lang="it"></span>


*Rana della piscina circolare: dimostrare che l'equilibrio è raggiunto*

> Come illustrato nella figura 3, un pool circolare è diviso in $2n$ ($n \geq 5$) "griglie". Si dice che due griglie siano vicine se hanno un lato comune o un arco. Quando le rane saltano contemporaneamente nelle griglie (con almeno una rane per griglia), si verifica una "erupzione" in una griglia se ci sono almeno tre rane in essa. Dopo un'eruzione in una griglia, una rana salta da quella griglia in ciascuna delle sue griglie vicine. Prova che dopo numerosi passi finiti, la distribuzione delle rane sarà in equilibrio (cioè non si verifica alcuna eruzione). (Posato da Su Chun)

*Sequence recurrence; prove inequality for n<=m*

> Let $\{a_n\}$ be a sequence such that $a_1 = \dfrac{21}{16}$ and
> $$2a_n - 3a_{n-1} = \frac{3}{2^{n+1}}, \quad n \geq 2.$$
> Let $m$ be a positive integer and $m \geq 2$. Prove that for $n \leq m$,
> $$\left(a_n + \frac{3}{2^{n+3}}\right)^{\frac{1}{m}}\left(m - \left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{\frac{n(m-1)}{m}}\right) < \frac{m^2 - 1}{m - n + 1}.$$
> (Posed by Zhu Huawei)

*5 points in unit rectangle; minimum triangles of area at most 1/4*

> There are 5 points in a rectangle $ABCD$ (including its boundary) with unit area such that any three of them are not collinear. Find the minimum number of triangles with areas no more than $\dfrac{1}{4}$ and vertices chosen from these 5 points. (Posed by Leng Gangsong)
