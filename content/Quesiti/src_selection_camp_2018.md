*Only finitely many subsets S of {1,...,n} with |S|≥√n+1 and all products xy being perfect powers*

> Prove that there are only finitely many positive integers $n$ for which there exists a subset $S \subseteq \{1, 2, \ldots, n\}$ satisfying the following two conditions:
> \begin{itemize}
> \item The number of elements of $S$ is at least $[\sqrt{n}] + 1$.
> \item For any two elements $x, y \in S$, the product $xy$ is a perfect power, i.e., an integer that can be expressed as $a^b$ for some positive integer $a$ and integer $b \ge 2$.
> \end{itemize}
> Here, for a real number $r$, $[r]$ denotes the greatest integer not exceeding $r$.

> For finite sets $X$ and $Y$ of positive integers, let $f_X(k)$ denote the $k$-th smallest positive integer not in $X$. Define
> $$X * Y = X \cup \{f_X(y) \mid y \in Y\}.$$
> Let $a, b$ be positive integers. Let $A$ be a set of $a$ positive integers and $B$ be a set of $b$ positive integers. Suppose $A * B = B * A$. Prove that
> $$\underbrace{A * (A * \cdots * (A * (A * A)) \cdots)}_{A \text{ appears } b \text{ times}} = \underbrace{B * (B * \cdots * (B * (B * B)) \cdots)}_{B \text{ appears } a \text{ times}}.$$

*Convex hexagon with AB=BC; reflections of A,B,C across line l; circumcircles meet at E; BB1 and DE meet on circumcircle*

> Let $ABCC_1B_1A_1$ be a convex hexagon satisfying $AB = BC$. Let $A_1$, $B_1$, $C_1$ be the reflections of $A$, $B$, $C$ respectively across a line $\ell$. Let $D$ be the intersection of segments $AC_1$ and $A_1C$, and let $\omega$ be the circumcircle of triangle $ABC$. Suppose $\omega$ and the circumcircle of triangle $A_1BC_1$ meet at a point $E$ other than $B$. Prove that line $BB_1$ and line $DE$ meet on $\omega$.

*Esagono converso con AB=BC; riflessi di A,B,C attraverso la linea l; circoncircoli si incontrano a E; BB1 e DE si incontrano su circoncircolo*

> Che $ABCC_1B_1A_1$ sia un esagono convexo che soddisfi $AB = BC$. $A_1$, $B_1$, $C_1$ siano rispettivamente i riflessi di $A$, $B$, $C$ su una linea $\ell$. Il $D$ è l'intersezione dei segmenti $AC_1$ e $A_1C$, e il $\omega$ è il circoncircolo del triangolo $ABC$. Supponiamo che $\omega$ e il circoncircolo del triangolo $A_1BC_1$ si incontrino in un punto $E$ diverso da $B$. Provare che la riga $BB_1$ e la riga $DE$ si incontrano su $\omega$.
