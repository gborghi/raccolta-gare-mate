*Infinite set of integers 2^k - 3 with no common prime factor*

> There is an infinite set of positive integers of the form $2^k - 3$ with the property $Q$: no two members of the set have a common prime factor. An outline of a proof is as follows.
> 
> Suppose there is a finite set $S = \{2^{r_1} - 3,\ 2^{r_2} - 3,\ \ldots\}$ with property $Q$ and having $k$ members. Let the prime factors of these $k$ numbers be $p_1, p_2, \ldots, p_j$. Consider the number $N = 2^{(p_1-1)(p_2-1)\cdots(p_j-1)} + 1$. By Fermat's theorem $2^{p-1} \equiv 1 \pmod{p}$ for every prime $p$ that does not divide $a$. Hence $N \equiv -3 + 3 \equiv 1 \pmod{p_s}$, $s = 1$ to $r$, and $N - 3$ may be added to $S$ to give a larger set with property $Q$.
> 
> Give a properly expanded and reasoned proof that there is an infinite set of positive integers of the form $2^k - 3$ with property $Q$.

>

> Date una prova adeguatamente ampliata e motivata che vi sia un insieme infinito di interi positivi della forma $2^k - 3$ con proprietà $Q$.

> (i) Two uniform rough right circular cylinders $A$ and $B$, with the same length, have radii and masses $a$, $b$ and $M$, $m$ respectively.
> 
> $A$ rests with a generator in contact with a rough horizontal table. $B$ rests on $A$, initially in unstable equilibrium, with its axis vertically above $A$'s. Equilibrium is disturbed, $B$ rolls on $A$ and $A$ rolls on the table. In the subsequent motion the plane containing the axes makes an angle $\theta$ with the vertical.
> 
> Draw diagrams showing angles, forces etc., for the period when there is no slipping. Write down equations which will give on elimination a differential equation for $\theta$, stating the principles used. Indicate how the elimination could be done; you are not asked to do it.
> 
> (ii) Such a differential equation is, with $k = m/M$,
> $$\ddot{\theta}\left(4 + 2\cos\alpha - 2\cos^2\alpha + 9\tfrac{k}{2}\right) + \dot{\theta}^2 \sin\alpha\,(2\cos\alpha - 1) = \frac{3g\,(1 + k)\sin\alpha}{a + b}.$$
> 
> Obtain $\dot{\theta}$ in terms of $\alpha$.
> 
> [Moment of inertia of a uniform cylinder about its axis is $\frac{1}{2}\,(\text{mass})(\text{radius})^2$.]
