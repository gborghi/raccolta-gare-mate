*Triangle with perpendicular foot and equal area regions implies BD=DC*

> Let $ABC$ be a triangle and let $AD$ be the perpendicular from $A$ to $BC$. Let $K$, $L$, $M$ be points on $AD$ such that $AK = KL = LM = MD$. If the sum of the areas of the shaded regions is equal to the sum of the areas of the unshaded regions, prove that $BD = DC$.

*Arithmetic progression: express common difference d in terms of x, y, z*

> Let $a_1, a_2, \ldots, a_{2n}$ be an arithmetic progression of positive real numbers with common difference $d$. Let
> (i) $a_1^2 + a_3^2 + \cdots + a_{2n-1}^2 = x$,
> (ii) $a_2^2 + a_4^2 + \cdots + a_{2n}^2 = y$, and
> (iii) $a_n + a_{n+1} = z$.
> Express $d$ in terms of $x$, $y$, $z$, $n$.

*Digits of 2^r permuted give 2^s; prove r=s*

> Suppose for some positive integers $r$ and $s$, the digits of $2^r$ is obtained by permuting the digits of $2^s$ in decimal expansion. Prove that $r = s$.

*Acute triangle, orthocentre, circumcircles: find point X independent of P*

> Let $ABC$ be an acute-angled triangle and let $H$ be its ortho-centre. For any point $P$ on the circum-circle of triangle $ABC$, let $Q$ be the point of intersection of the line $BH$ with the line $AP$. Show that there is a unique point $X$ on the circum-circle of $ABC$ such that for every point $P \neq A, B$, the circum-circle of $HQP$ passes through $X$.

**Topic:** [[topic_geometria_piana|Geometria piana]]
**Metodo:** [[method_coordinate|Coordinate]]

<div class="qlang-split" data-lang="it"></div>


*Triangolo acuto, ortocentro, circonferenze circoscritte: trovare il punto X indipendentemente da P*

> Sia $ABC$ un triangolo acutangolo e sia $H$ il suo ortocentro. Per ogni punto $P$ sulla circonferenza circoscritta al triangolo $ABC$, sia $Q$ il punto di intersezione della retta $BH$ con la retta $AP$. Dimostrare che esiste un unico punto $X$ sulla circonferenza circoscritta a $ABC$ tale che, per ogni punto $P \neq A, B$, la circonferenza circoscritta a $HQP$ passi per $X$.

*Positive reals summing to 1; find smallest K for sum inequality*

> Let $x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_{2014}$ be positive real numbers such that $\sum_{j=1}^{2014} x_j = 1$. Determine with proof the smallest constant $K$ such that
> $$K \sum_{j=1}^{2014} \frac{x_j^2}{1 - x_j} \ge 1.$$
